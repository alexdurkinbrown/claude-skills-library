# Website Copy Architect

**How to structure and write a home page for a service-based business, section by section, within the exact lengths the design allows.**

This skill is for personal brands, consultants, coaches, and any service-based business. It is built on the home page framework used in agency client builds, so it carries the real section order and the strict character counts that copy has to hit. It covers the home page for now, with the about, offers, and content pages to follow later. Pair it with your writing rules for the sentence-level work, so the structure here stays human on the page and never reads as generated.

---

## The Non-Negotiables

- Character counts are hard limits, and they include spaces and punctuation. They come from sales-copy experience and from what fits the design, so copy that runs over or under will break the layout. Hit the range for every field, every time.
- Word counts on short fields are also firm. A two to four word CTA stays two to four words.
- Never invent proof. Every number, name, testimonial, and credential is real and confirmed with the client, since invented proof is the fastest way to lose the client's trust and the visitor's.
- Apply the writing rules to every line. No AI slop, no em dashes, no contrast constructions, full flowing sentences.

---

## Before You Write: Client Intake

Gather these before drafting, since vague inputs produce generic copy and specifics are what make a home page convert.

- The ideal customer for this site, including the pain they arrive with and the outcome they want.
- The single primary action the home page should drive, such as booking a call, applying, or buying.
- The proof on hand: client results with real numbers, testimonials with names and titles, recognizable logos, and credentials.
- The offer specifics: what the main offering is, what is included, the price or price range, and how it is delivered.
- What makes this client different from the obvious alternatives, in plain words a visitor would understand.
- Brand voice notes, including the words the client loves and the words they will not use.

If any of these are missing, ask for them before writing rather than filling the gap with invented detail.

---

## Core Principles

- Clarity beats cleverness, especially in the hero. A stranger should understand who the business helps and what it does within about five seconds, so plain language wins over wordplay every time.
- Lead with the outcome the visitor wants, then show how the business delivers it. Benefits open a section, and features support them.
- Front-load the most important information. People scan top to bottom and rarely reach the end, so the strongest promise and the clearest proof sit high on the page.
- Write for skimmers. The headers carry the message on their own, and someone reading only the headers down the page should still understand the offer.
- Earn every section. If a section does not move the visitor closer to the primary action, cut it.
- Keep one primary action across the page. Buttons can repeat, and they all point to the same next step.

## Home Page, Section by Section

Sections 1 through 3 and 7 form the backbone. The content feed, freebie, newsletter, and Instagram sections are included only when the client actually has those assets.

### Section 1: Hero

The hero does the heavy lifting, so it has to pass the five-second test. It needs three things working together.

1. **Brand name or logo.** The header carries the brand name, or an informative statement about the brand when the name itself is the logo. (20 to 30 characters)
2. **The idiot-proof statement.** One simple sentence that says what the business is and what it does, in terms anyone could understand on the first read. No jargon, no cleverness, no inside language. A stranger should get it instantly. This is the hook, and it also names what makes the offering different. (75 to 150 characters)
3. **Subheader.** One or two sentences that expand the hook, give a high-level sense of the business, and pull the reader to keep scrolling. (140 to 250 characters)
4. **CTA.** A short, action-led button, placed in the hero or the nav bar, written so it stands out and gives the visitor one clear next step. (2 to 4 words)

Include the CTA link.

### Section 2: About

A brief introduction that builds trust and sends people to the full about page.

- **Pre-header.** A short label, for example "meet your guide." (2 to 4 words)
- **Header.** A short, personal opener, for example "hi, I'm [name]." (2 to 4 words)
- **Body.** One or two paragraphs covering who they are, their credentials, and why they do the work. Open with a strong personal statement, then zoom out for context. (250 to 500 characters)
- **CTA.** (2 to 4 words), plus the link.

### Section 2.5: Quote

- **Quote.** A powerful line from the client that captures their message and ethos. (80 to 90 characters)
- A full-bleed image accompanies it, linked if the client has a preference.

### Section 3: Main Offering

The headline offer, framed around results.

- **Pre-header.** For example "introducing." (2 to 4 words)
- **Header.** A short headline naming the offering. (2 to 4 words)
- **Subheader (optional).** One or two sentences on the results the offer delivers and what the client gets.
- **Body.** Three to four sentences describing the offering and its benefits, kept conversational and top-level. Distill what is most important and most enticing, and what improves in the client's life. (200 to 400 characters)
- **CTA.** (2 to 4 words), plus the link.

### Section 4: Content Feed (optional)

Use this only if the client has a podcast, YouTube channel, or blog.

- **Pre-header.** For example "the podcast." (2 to 4 words)
- **Header.** Names the channel. (2 to 4 words)
- **Subheader.** Labels the list, for example "featured episodes." (2 to 4 words)
- **Featured content.** A short list of episode or post titles with their links.
- **CTA.** For example "more episodes," routing to the content page or listening platform. (2 to 4 words), plus the link.

### Section 5: Offering Suite

A range of offers for different stages of the client's journey, up to three shown here.

- **Scrolling header.** For example "offering suite." (2 to 4 words)
- **Subheader.** One or two sentences on how the suite serves different segments of the audience. (100 to 250 characters)
- For each offer, up to three:
  - **Offer name.** (1 to 4 words)
  - **Body.** Benefit-led and results-focused, distilling what the client gets and how they transform in measurable and emotional terms. (150 to 400 characters)
  - **CTA.** For example "apply now," routing to that offer's page. (2 to 4 words), plus the link.

### Section 6: Freebie (optional)

Use this only if the client has a lead magnet.

- **Header.** A short headline for the freebie. (2 to 4 words)
- **Subheader.** The value inside, stated fast. (40 to 100 characters)
- **Body.** Benefit-led, distilling what people get in a nutshell. (150 to 350 characters)
- Include the freebie link.

### Section 7: Testimonials

Keep every testimonial roughly the same length so the design stays even.

- **Pre-header.** For example "what clients say." (2 to 4 words)
- **Header.** For example "client love" or "success stories." (1 to 2 words)
- For each testimonial, up to four:
  - A headshot or video link.
  - **Single-sentence call-out.** One pulled line that captures the result.
  - **Client feedback.** The testimonial itself. (240 to 250 characters)
  - The client's name and title.

### Section 8: Newsletter (optional)

- **Pre-header.** For example "get the newsletter." (2 to 4 words)
- **Header.** The opt-in name. (2 to 3 words)
- **Body.** Two or three value-based sentences. Where it fits, name the frequency and day, and list what the emails include. (150 to 350 characters)

### Section 9: Instagram Feed (optional)

- **Header.** An invitation to follow, for example "follow along on Instagram." (2 to 4 words)
- The Instagram handle.

## Page Checklist

Run this before the home page copy is final.

- [ ] Every field sits inside its character or word count, spaces and punctuation included
- [ ] The hero header carries the brand name or a clear brand statement
- [ ] The idiot-proof statement explains the business in one plain sentence a stranger would understand
- [ ] The primary CTA is short, action-led, and placed to stand out in the hero or nav
- [ ] The page drives one primary action, and every button points to it
- [ ] Copy leads with outcomes before features
- [ ] Someone reading only the headers down the page understands the offer
- [ ] Optional sections appear only when the client has that asset
- [ ] Testimonials are roughly equal in length
- [ ] Every number, name, and result is real and confirmed with the client
- [ ] The writing rules are applied to every line, with no AI slop, no em dashes, and no contrast constructions
