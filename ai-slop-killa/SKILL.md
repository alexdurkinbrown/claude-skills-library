# AI Slop Killa // Writing Rules

**The standard for making everything sound human.**

Every rule below exists to kill the patterns that give AI away and to make writing read like a real person talking, not a template being filled in. The order moves from the guiding principle, to the core rules, into the specific tells worth hunting down before anything ships.

---

## The North Star

Read it out loud. If it sounds written rather than said, rewrite it. If anyone could tell AI produced it, it does not publish. That is the only test that matters, and every rule that follows is just a way of passing it.

---

## The Core Rules

**01. Write in full sentences with natural flow.** Connect thoughts with commas and 'and' so ideas move into each other instead of stopping hard after every point. The target is an average of 15 to 25 words per sentence, varied so nothing reads uniform.

**02. No contrast constructions.** This is the single biggest tell to cut. State what something IS, directly, instead of defining it against what it is not. Covered in full in its own section below.

**03. The 20% rule.** After drafting, cut a fifth of the words. If it runs 500, get it to 400. Tight writing is human writing, and most first drafts are padded with phrases that carry no weight.

**04. No repetitive patterns.** Do not start consecutive paragraphs the same way, repeat an emphasis word, or run the same sentence structure back to back. Real writing is uneven, so vary the shape of every line.

**05. Rule of 3.** When listing, use one item or three, never two. Two items create that artificial paired-up feel, and three reads natural and complete.

**06. Do not overexplain.** Trust the reader. If the story is good, the insight lands without spelling it out, so delete 'the lesson here is' and 'what I learned was' and anything that summarizes the point you just made.

**07. Make it personal.** Use 'I', 'you', 'me', 'your'. Writing should feel like a conversation with one person who gets it, not an article or a report written to a crowd.

**08. No filler phrases.** Every banned phrase exists because it adds zero value, so state the thing directly instead of clearing your throat before it.

**09. Cut the coach-y transitions.** Lines like 'here's the thing', 'let me explain', and 'and that brings me to' announce a turn instead of making it. Make the turn and move.

**10. Bullets earn their place.** Use them only when they genuinely improve readability, like a real list of steps or tools. Default to flowing prose rather than chopping every thought into its own line.

**11. No line break overload.** Not every sentence needs its own line, and a piece built entirely from single-line zingers feels performative and constructed. Let some sentences live together in a paragraph.

**12. Specificity is the proof.** Replace every vague word with an exact number, name, or date. Detail is what proves a human was actually there, and it is the cheapest credibility you can add.

## Sentence Rhythm & Flow

Uniform sentence length is the number one AI tell, in both directions. AI either writes everything choppy and staccato, or everything in the same medium 10 to 15 word cadence, and both patterns are recognizable on sight.

The fix is flow with deliberate variety, held inside a working range that averages 15 to 25 words.

- Connect thoughts with 'and' and commas so ideas unspool into each other rather than stopping after every beat.
- Let sentences breathe when you are building an image or making a point, then settle into a tighter one to keep pace.
- Vary the lengths on purpose so no two neighbors match, which is what makes prose feel spoken instead of generated.

What this rules out, every time:

- No sentence fragments. Every line is a complete sentence with a subject and a verb.
- No short sentences dropped in for dramatic punch. Emphasis comes from what you say, not from clipping a thought down to three words.
- No starting a sentence with 'And' or 'But' to manufacture a beat. Fold the connector inside the sentence instead.
- No staccato stacking, where three or four tiny sentences pile up to sound poetic.

### The tell, and the fix

> AI pattern: "I made a plan. I stuck to it. I saw results."

Three identical short sentences stacked in a row read like marketing copy. Rewritten with flow: "I made a plan and stuck to it, and the results showed up a few weeks later."

## No Contrast Constructions

State what something IS. Never define it by parking it next to its opposite for cheap effect. This pattern is the fastest way to sound like a quote graphic, and it shows up in more disguises than people realize.

Banned in every form:

- "it's not X, it's Y"
- "this isn't X, it's Y"
- "it's X, not Y"
- "not just X, but Y" and "not only X but also Y"
- Balanced parallel setups like "she didn't just lead, she transformed"
- Antithesis headers stacked together, like "Strategy, not luck." then "Skill, not talent." then "Habits, not hacks." One reads fine, three in a row is a fingerprint.
- Clever contrast closings like "you don't run the business, the business runs you"

The fix is always the same. Cut the negative half and say the real thing outright.

> Instead of "this isn't a quick fix, it's a long-term shift," write "this works slowly, and the changes hold because they build on each other."

---

## No Rhetorical Questions

Make statements. Tell the reader what you see instead of asking them to feel something on your behalf. No 'Sound familiar?', no 'What if you could...?', and no questions used as a transition between ideas. Questions are a crutch that hand the work over to the reader rather than doing it on the page.

The one exception is a genuine mirror question that makes someone examine their own behavior, like "what would I keep doing even if no one were watching?" That is real self-reflection, not engagement bait, so use it rarely and only when the content has earned it.

## Banned Punctuation

| Never use | Use instead |
|-----------|-------------|
| Em dashes (the long dash) | Commas or a line break |
| Double dashes (--) | Commas or a line break |
| Semicolons | Periods or commas, keep it simple |
| Oxford commas | Drop the comma before the final 'and' |
| Colons in titles or headers | Rephrase so the title stands on its own |
| Trailing ellipses | Only as a connector between two related ideas, never to trail off |

The ellipsis works when it links two connected thoughts ("the first launch flopped...the second one paid for the whole year"). It fails the moment it dissolves a sentence into nothing ("I just don't know..."). Connecting is allowed, fading out is banned.

## Banned Words

### AI filler qualifiers, the biggest word-level tells

genuinely, honestly, actually, really, truly. These add nothing and signal generated copy on contact. Let the sentence carry its own weight.

### AI vocabulary red flags

delve, tapestry, multifaceted, nuanced, resonate, embark, journey, foster, illuminate, paramount, pivotal, robust, seamless, holistic, cutting-edge, revolutionary, testament, meticulous, intricate, comprehensive, furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition, in essence, in conclusion, in summary.

### Corporate and buzzword speak

unpack, deep dive, pivot, leverage (as a verb), utilize, game changer, moat, something shifted, intentional community building, sitting at a high vibration.

### Woo-woo and spiritual fluff

aligned, container, vibes, portal, divine feminine, drop in, this energy. These read as vague and mystical, and they fill space without saying anything.

### Bossbabe and girlboss culture

babe, girly, iconic. This vocabulary dates the writing and reads as performance.

### Bro marketing lingo

grind, beast mode, level up, chaos as a flex.

### Gen Z slang

bet, giving, ate, hits different, receipts.

## Banned Phrases

| Phrase | What to do instead |
|--------|--------------------|
| here's the thing | Just state the thing |
| let me explain | Just explain |
| the truth is | Just state the truth |
| it's not about X, it's about Y | State what it IS about, directly |
| at the end of the day | Delete entirely |
| let that sink in | Delete, trust the reader |
| something shifted | Name exactly what changed |
| and that brings me to | Delete, just make the point |
| here's what I want you to understand | Delete, just state it |
| I want to talk about something important | Delete, it's throat-clearing |
| sound familiar | Delete, it's engagement bait |
| more than ever | Delete, or be specific about when |
| in the first place | Delete, it's filler |
| it's safe to say | Delete, just say it |

## Structural AI Tells to Hunt Down

### Repetition

Watch for any emphasis word used twice in one piece, the same phrase resurfacing across paragraphs, or formulaic progressions where each section opens identically. All three are fingerprints.

### Rhythm problems

Beyond uniform length, watch for dual-adjective stacking like "bold and brave" or "smart and strategic," where two paired words do the work of one strong one. Pick the single best word and drop the pair. Watch too for punch-punch-punch endings, where imperative commands stack up at the close like a motivational poster.

### Structural patterns

Stacked one-liner openings, a run of isolated single sentences each on its own line at the top, signal AI every time, because real writing opens with connected thought. The same goes for repeated antithesis headers and clever constructed closings, both covered in the contrast section above.

### Over-narration

Cut anything that announces a turn instead of taking it: "and then everything changed," "something shifted," or any sentence whose only job is to warn that an important point is coming. Show the scene rather than explaining the emotion, and never summarize what the writing already landed. The worst version of this is the philosophical summary bow, a tidy one-liner that wraps the whole piece in a package. If a piece needs a summary sentence to land, it did not land, so cut it.

### Vague language

Replace 'more than ever' and 'like never before' with a real timeframe, and swap "saved a lot of time" for "saved six hours a week." Generic meaningful-sounding phrases that fill space without carrying a real detail are dead weight.

## Specificity, the Human Proof

Vague language reads as AI, and exact detail proves a person was there. If you can be specific and you are not, you are leaving credibility on the table.

| Vague | Specific |
|-------|----------|
| recently | last Tuesday |
| a tool | Google Docs |
| a lot of money | $12,000 |
| a significant amount of time | six hours a week |
| a big outcome | tripled the list in four months |
| some people | the nine of us in the room |
| sometime soon | March 4th |

## Endings, Warm Not Punchy

AI endings do one of two things, and both are wrong. They stack commands ("do the thing, make the call, book the meeting") or they wrap up in a constructed philosophical one-liner. Real endings feel like advice from someone who has actually been there, so they flow, they often carry a parenthetical aside, and they connect to something bigger.

- No punch-punch-punch command closings, they read like a poster.
- No philosophical bow, if the piece needed a one-liner to land, it did not land.
- Default to stopping when the point lands rather than reaching for more.
- The best closings sound like a friend talking, not a closing argument.

## Master Pre-Publish Checklist

### Voice quality
- [ ] Sounds like a human talking, not a template being filled in
- [ ] Would be said out loud naturally
- [ ] No sentence trying to sound smart, poetic, or constructed
- [ ] Nobody could tell AI wrote it

### Words
- [ ] No filler qualifiers (genuinely, honestly, actually, really, truly)
- [ ] No AI vocabulary (delve, tapestry, nuanced, resonate, paramount)
- [ ] No corporate, woo-woo, bossbabe, bro, or Gen Z slang
- [ ] No emphasis word repeated more than once

### Structure
- [ ] No contrast constructions in any form
- [ ] No rhetorical or engagement-bait questions
- [ ] No em dashes, semicolons, or Oxford commas
- [ ] Sentences vary in length, average 15 to 25 words, no fragments
- [ ] No staccato stacking or short-for-drama lines
- [ ] No coach-y transitions announcing a turn
- [ ] No stacked one-liner openings or repeated antithesis headers
- [ ] Ending is warm and flowing, no command stack, no philosophical bow

### Specificity
- [ ] Vague words swapped for exact numbers, names, or dates
- [ ] All claims and figures accurate and verified
- [ ] At least one concrete detail proving firsthand experience

### Assumptions
- [ ] No gender role, family structure, or financial assumptions
- [ ] No invented details about real people

## Quick Reference

| Never do | Always do |
|----------|-----------|
| Ask rhetorical or bait questions | Make declarative statements |
| Use genuinely / honestly / really / truly | Let the writing carry itself |
| Use em dashes, semicolons, Oxford commas | Use commas and line breaks |
| Write fragments or short-for-drama lines | Full sentences, 15 to 25 word average |
| Define things by their opposite | State what it IS, directly |
| Over-explain emotions | Show what happened, let the reader feel it |
| Summarize what you just said | Stop when the point lands |
| Stack one-liners at the top | Open with connected, flowing thought |
| Repeat the same header structure | Vary header styles throughout |
| End with stacked commands or a neat bow | Close warm, grounded, and flowing |
| Use vague language | Use exact numbers, names, dates |
| Default to bullets | Use bullets only when they help, prose otherwise |
